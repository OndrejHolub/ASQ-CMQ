The 100-Year Quality Evolution

Summary

Quality management as a discipline emerged from three converging streams — statistical quality control, productivity improvement, and information technology — spanning roughly one century. Understanding the chronology, key figures, and defining concepts of each era is foundational to the entire CMQ/OE Body of Knowledge.

1. Chronological Overview

The evolution of quality management follows a broadly linear progression, though each methodology built upon rather than replaced its predecessors.

Statistical Process Control (SPC) — 1920s
Developed by Walter Shewhart at Western Electric / Bell Telephone Laboratories. Uses control charts to distinguish assignable-cause variation (correctable) from chance-cause variation (inherent to the process). Foundation of all subsequent data-driven quality methods.
Total Quality Management (TQM) — 1980s
Organization-wide continuous improvement. Characterized by customer focus, data-driven decision-making, and involvement of all employees. Popularized by the American response to Japanese manufacturing dominance.
Business Process Reengineering (BPR) — Late 1980s
Associated with Hammer & Champy. Advocates radical redesign of core processes from the ground up, discarding existing structures rather than incrementally improving them. Often linked to major IT implementations.
Six Sigma — 1990s
Developed at Motorola, scaled at GE under Jack Welch. Targets 3.4 defects per million opportunities (DPMO). Uses the DMAIC problem-solving methodology (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control).
Business Process Management (BPM) — Modern
Integrated, cross-functional process management. BPM processes are: standardized, measurable, repeatable, and reusable. Incorporates Lean-Six Sigma and crosses departmental and divisional boundaries.

The CMQ/OE examination requires specific knowledge of each pioneer's contributions. Conflating their work is a common error.

Deming's System of Profound Knowledge

Deming's overarching framework comprises four interrelated components:

  1. Appreciation for a system
  2. Knowledge of variation
  3. Theory of knowledge
  4. Psychology

3. Three Converging Streams

Stream 1 — Productivity

Ford introduced the assembly line and interchangeable parts. The Toyota Production System (TPS) added continuous flow, pull systems, and respect for workers. Just-in-Time (JIT) manufacturing produces only what is needed, when needed, in the amount needed, eliminating inventory waste. Lean (JIT + TPS) targets the seven wastes: overproduction, waiting, transport, over-processing, inventory, motion, and defects.

Stream 3 — Quality

The quality stream evolved from Shewhart's control charts through TQM, Six Sigma, and ultimately merged with the productivity and IT streams to form modern BPM practice.

Exam Key Point

SBPA (Strategic Business Process Architecture) is a systems engineering approach aligning strategy across CMO, CIO, and COO functions. It represents the convergence of all three streams.

4. Industry Spread

Quality management originated in manufacturing but has since expanded into: healthcare, financial services, retail, education, and government. The underlying principles are industry-agnostic.
